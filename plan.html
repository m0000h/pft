Overall goal

Build a sustainable daily rhythm that syncs with your early schedule — helping you fall asleep faster, stay asleep longer, and wake up genuinely refreshed. The focus is on small, consistent habits that compound over time, not overnight fixes.

Key principles

Consistency
Same wake and bed times every day — including weekends. Your body clock is trained by regularity above all else.
Light exposure
Morning sunlight anchors your circadian rhythm. Evening screen light delays it. Both matter enormously.
Sleep pressure
Adenosine builds all day. Protect it — avoid long naps and caffeine after midday.
Wind-down ritual
Your nervous system needs a transition signal. A consistent 60–90 min pre-bed routine trains your body to shift into sleep mode.

Daily schedule

4:45 am
Wake up — consistent every day
Same time 7 days a week. The single most important anchor. Get up on the first alarm.
4:45–5:30
Morning routine
Drink 2 glasses of water immediately. Step outside briefly for early light exposure. Light breakfast. Hold caffeine until 5:00 am minimum.
5:45 am
Leave for work
Outdoor or windowed commute provides valuable light exposure. Avoid heavy meals before departure.
At work
Workday energy management
Stay hydrated. Move every 60–90 min. Last caffeine by 10:00–10:30 am. Protein-rich lunch at midday prevents afternoon crashes.
3:00 pm
Home — decompress first
Change clothes, drink water, spend 10–15 min doing nothing demanding. Signal to your body that work mode is ending.
3:30–5:00
Exercise window
Ideal slot — body temp and cortisol return to baseline well before bed. See the Exercise page for your full plan.
5:30–6:30
Dinner
Main meal here — at least 2.5 hours before bed. Avoid large, heavy, or spicy meals after 6:30 pm. See Meal Plan for ideas.
7:00 pm
Begin dimming environment
Switch to lamps or warm-toned bulbs. Avoid stressful news, work emails, or heated conversations.
7:30–8:15
Wind-down ritual
Screens off. Stretching, reading, breathing. See Wind-down Stretches page for the full routine.
8:30 pm
Target bedtime — lights out
8 hours before your 4:45 am wake-up. No screens, no phone. If sleep doesn't come in 20 min, do a quiet activity in dim light then return.

Sleep environment

Temperature
17–19°C
Core body temp must drop to initiate sleep. Use a fan — Perth summers may need A/C.
Light
Complete darkness
Blackout curtains or a quality sleep mask. Cover all LED standby lights.
Sound
Quiet or white noise
Silence is ideal. Earplugs or a white noise machine to mask unavoidable noise.
Bed use
Sleep & sex only
No TV, phone, or work in bed. Train your brain to associate bed exclusively with sleep.

Diet & hydration

EatTryptophan-rich foods at dinner: chicken, turkey, eggs, dairy, nuts. These are melatonin precursors.
DrinkChamomile tea or tart cherry juice in the evening. Aim for 2–3 L of water across the day.
AvoidCaffeine after 10:00–10:30 am. With an 8:30 pm bedtime and a 6-hour half-life, later caffeine is still active at bedtime.
AvoidAlcohol within 3 hours of bed — it fragments sleep, suppresses REM, and causes early waking.
AvoidLarge, heavy, or spicy meals after 6:30 pm. Digestion raises core body temperature and disrupts sleep onset.

Daytime fatigue management

NappingIf needed, a 10–20 min nap between 1:00–2:30 pm is safe. Set an alarm — shorter naps protect your sleep pressure.
AvoidNever nap after 3:00 pm or for longer than 30 min. This directly reduces your drive to sleep at 8:30 pm.
TipThe 2:00–3:00 pm dip is biological. Combat it with a short walk, cold water on the face, or a movement break — not caffeine.
TipDehydration of just 1–2% significantly worsens alertness. Sip water consistently — don't wait until you're thirsty.

Consistency, patience & when to seek help
Most people notice meaningful improvement within 5–7 days. Full circadian adaptation takes 2–3 weeks. Make only one adjustment at a time and wait 3–5 days to assess it. If fatigue and poor sleep persist beyond 2–3 weeks despite consistent effort, please consult a GP — sleep apnoea or another underlying condition may need investigation.

Key principles for belly fat loss
High protein (1.6–2g per kg body weight) preserves muscle while losing fat. Fibre from vegetables and legumes specifically reduces visceral fat. Avoid all sugary drinks. Eat dinner by 6:30 pm — this allows overnight fat metabolism to begin well before sleep. The single biggest lever is reducing ultra-processed foods and added sugar, not eliminating food groups.

Great starting point. Fat-burning zone. Perfect for Perth's flat paths and river walks.

Low joint impact, high calorie burn. Heart rate at 60–70% max — able to hold a conversation.

Jumping jacks, high knees, mountain climbers, squat jumps. No equipment. Most effective for belly fat loss.

Outdoor or treadmill. Produces EPOC — your body burns fat for hours after the session ends.
Skipping (jump rope)

Cheap, effective, excellent fat-burner. One of the best calorie-per-minute exercises available.

Why compound strength beats crunches for belly fat
Spot reduction is a myth. Compound movements (squats, deadlifts, push-ups) build muscle across your whole body, raising your resting metabolic rate so you burn more fat around the clock. Combined with HIIT twice a week and the dietary changes, this is the fastest evidence-based route to reducing visceral abdominal fat.
How to use this routine (20–25 min, 7:30–8:00 pm)
Do this in a dimly lit room on a mat. Move slowly, breathe deeply through each hold. Never force a stretch — gentle tension only. This sequence is ordered to lower cortisol, release physical tension, and signal your nervous system that sleep is coming. Do the full routine on rest days; on exercise days, prioritise steps 1, 3, 6 and 9 as a minimum.
1
Child's pose
90 sec
Kneel, sit back onto heels, reach arms forward, forehead on the mat. Deep belly breathing. Releases lower back and hips — areas that tighten from physical work and long days on your feet.
2
Supine knee-to-chest
60 sec each side
Lie on your back. Pull one knee gently toward your chest, hold, then switch. Releases the lumbar spine and piriformis. Especially useful if you're on your feet for much of the workday.
3
Supine spinal twist
60 sec each side
Lying on your back, bring one knee across your body and let it fall to the opposite side. Arms out in a T. Keep both shoulders on the floor. Decompresses the spine and activates the parasympathetic nervous system.
4
Seated forward fold
90 sec
Sit with legs straight. Hinge from the hips and reach toward your feet. Let your head and neck hang heavy. Keep a slight bend in the knees if tight. Stretches hamstrings and lower back.
5
Hip flexor lunge stretch
60 sec each side
From kneeling, step one foot forward into a lunge. Sink hips gently forward and downward. Hip flexors tighten significantly with early starts and physical work. Releasing them reduces overnight muscle tension and lower back pain.
6
Legs-up-the-wall
3–5 min
Lie on your back with legs resting vertically up a wall, hips close to the wall, arms relaxed at your sides. Promotes venous return, lowers heart rate, and is clinically associated with faster sleep onset. The single most effective pre-sleep position known.
7
Reclining butterfly
90 sec
Lie on your back, bring soles of feet together and let knees fall out to the sides. Hands on your belly or at your sides. Releases inner thighs, groin, and hips. Try to make your exhale longer than your inhale.
8
Neck & shoulder release
30 sec × 4 directions
Seated or lying. Slowly tilt: ear to shoulder, chin to chest, gentle left and right rotations. Never roll the head backward. Hold 30 seconds each. Relieves tension headaches and jaw clenching that often accompanies fatigue.
9
4-7-8 breathing (closing)
5 cycles
Lie flat. Inhale through the nose for 4 counts, hold for 7 counts, exhale slowly through the mouth for 8 counts. Repeat 5 times. Activates the vagus nerve and reliably lowers heart rate within minutes. Do this last — then go straight to bed.
